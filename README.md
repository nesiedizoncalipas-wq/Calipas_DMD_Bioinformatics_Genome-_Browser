## **7. Interpretation**

The selected variant, NM_004006.3(DMD).2949+2T>C, occurs at a canonical splice-donor site in intron 22 of DMD. ClinVar identifies its molecular consequence as a splice-donor variant.
Because the variant affects a splice-donor site, it may interfere with normal processing of DMD pre-mRNA. ClinVar submissions describe the variant as potentially producing abnormal RNA splicing and an altered or disrupted dystrophin protein.
The current ClinVar aggregate classification is Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic, based on the submitted clinical evidence.
However, genomic location alone is not enough to determine the complete effect of a variant. Additional evidence such as RNA/splicing studies, protein studies, clinical information, family segregation, population-frequency data, and other genetic evidence can provide stronger support for interpreting the variant.

## **8. Reflection**

### **1. What did UCSC show you about your gene that was not obvious from simply reading about thegene's function?**

UCSC showed the physical organization of the DMD gene within the human genome. It allowed the genomic location, exon-intron structure, transcript models, and additional genomic annotations to be examined directly.

### **2.  Why is knowing the exact genomic location of a disease-associated variant useful?**

Knowing the exact genomic location allows a variant to be connected to a specific gene and genomic feature such as an exon, intron, UTR, or splice region. It also allows the variant to be compared with other genome annotations and known variants.

### **3. What is one limitation of predicting a variant's effect only from its genomic location?**

The genomic location alone does not show exactly how a variant affects RNA, protein function, or an individual's phenotype. Additional experimental and clinical evidence is needed to understand its biological and disease-related effects.

### **4. What was the most interesting feature you observed about your assigned gene?**

One interesting feature of DMD is its very large genomic span on the X chromosome and its multiple transcript models. Another interesting feature is that a variant located in an intronic splice region can potentially affect the production of the dystrophin protein.
